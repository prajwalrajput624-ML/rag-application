The Kachhwaha (Sanskrit: कछवाहा, lit. 'tortoise') is a Rajput clan from India. Kachhwaha genealogical traditions trace the clan's ancestry to Kusha, the mythological son of Rama, a central figure of the Vaishnava epic Ramayana. Families belonging to the clan established their rule in the Dhundhar region and later ruled the Kingdom of Amber, before establishing Jaipur as their new capital in 1727.

Etymology
According to Cynthia Talbot, Kachhwaha is a Sanskrit word which means tortoise. According to other sources, the clan name ‘Kacchwaha’ has been derived from the name of Kusha, Rama’s son, so they also claim descent from mythical hero Rama’s son Kusha and thus from the Solar dynasty

Origin
Kachhwaha genealogical traditions trace the ancestry of the dynasty to Kusha, the mythological son of the Vaishnava god Rama, and consequently place the dynasty within the mythological Solar dynasty. Later historical accounts have proposed different explanations for the emergence of the dynasty. According to James Mill, the ancestors of the Kachhwaha Rajputs migrated from Kosala and established a ruling house at Gwalior, before subsequently moving into Rajasthan in 1028 CE. Some historians have also sought to associate Dulha Rao, regarded in Kachhwaha tradition as the founder of the Kachhwaha ruling house of Amber, with the Kachchhapaghata dynasty, which exercised authority in parts of central India between the 10th and 12th centuries.
The dynasty is recorded under several names and forms in historical and genealogical sources. According to Rima Hooja, variants including Kachhapaghata, Kachwaha, Katsawaha and Kachawa occur in inscriptions, manuscripts and later genealogical traditions. The form Kachawa became increasingly prominent during the late 16th century, during the reign of Raja Man Singh, a Mughal Subedar. Inscriptions and manuscripts from Balvan, Chatsu, Sanganer and Rewasa have also been cited in discussions of the early history, nomenclature and genealogy of the Kachhwahas.

Sub-Clans
There are approximately 71 subclans of the Kachhwahas. The prominent ones are:

Rajawat - The Rajawats hold the right of succession to the Jaipur throne, and the Maharaja of Jaipur belongs to this subclan.
Shekhawat - Descendants of Rao shekha.
Naruka - Descendants of Rao Naru .
Nathawat - Descendants of rao natha.
Khangarot- Descendants of Rao khangar.
